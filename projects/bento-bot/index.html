Bento Bot

Yohta Kitagawa, Yuki Horiuchi · 2026

A robotic lunchbox that invites people to share a picnic from where they are.

A robotic lunchbox that invites people to share a picnic from where they are.

A picnic meant to welcome newcomers can still look like a private gathering. At a park on the University of Tsukuba campus, we set up a picnic beside a walking route. Bento Bot carries some of the snacks being shared on the tarp toward people who pass by. It stops on the grass and plays a short invitation. Someone who takes a snack can eat where they stopped, join the group on the tarp, or keep walking.

We call this Actuated Commoning. An object already in use in a shared activity moves beyond the place where that activity began. Can the patch of grass where someone stops become part of the same picnic?

Technical Details

  • A 360° camera housed in a water bottle sends video to a computer in the picnic bag, which estimates the visitor’s direction.
  • The computer sends motor commands wirelessly to the Arduino in the lunchbox’s wheeled base.
  • A smartphone mounted on the box estimates proximity with its camera and plays a recorded invitation when the box stops.

Actuated Commoning. The lunchbox moves from the picnic toward people passing by, making room for an encounter beyond the tarp.

Process

Our first picnic prototype localized itself using QR codes printed on the tarp. As soon as the box reached the grass, it lost its reference. We moved the camera to a water bottle that stays with the group, giving the box room to travel while keeping both parts in familiar picnic forms.

In informal trials, people often sat beside the lunchbox without moving onto the tarp. One visitor began eating there, noticed the group, and asked what the lunchbox was. Some people read the size of the tarp as a cue for how many people were expected to join. These observations shaped our next question for field trials: when someone eats beside the box, do they feel that they are taking part in the same picnic?
