How can wired earphones weave tangible, embodied, and entangled experiences?

Knot-music remakes everyday objects in wired earphones. The same cords can carry an audio signal and form the weave of a hat, a lock of hair, or a hanging blind. When the earpieces play, the shape and movement of the object affect how their sound reaches the body and the room.

The series began as a performance of untangling earphones. It now includes works worn or carried around the body and works placed among familiar things in a room.

Knot-music performance

  • Three MP3 players drive three wired earphones.
  • Four microphones around the performance area capture sound leakage and handling noise.
  • Each microphone feeds the matching loudspeaker in a four-channel array.
  • The earphones' physical positions become the spatial mix.

Process

Knot-music began with the everyday act of untangling earphones. For the first iteration, playing three different tracks through three entangled pairs turned the task of untangling into a spatial composition that changes with every pull and knot.

The work moved from untangling loose cables to weaving them into familiar forms. Around the body, a hat holds sound in place while a gloved hand can move individual earpieces. In a room, the weave of a blind holds the earpieces at the threshold between indoors and outdoors.
